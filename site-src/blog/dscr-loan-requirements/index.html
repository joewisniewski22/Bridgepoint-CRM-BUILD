DSCR Loan Requirements: What Lenders Actually Look At

Credit, down payment, reserves and the ratio itself, explained without the jargon.

By the BridgePoint Lending team · October 4, 2026

DSCR loans are popular because they skip the personal income paperwork. That does not mean there are no requirements. Every lender sets its own guidelines, but the same handful of factors shows up almost everywhere. Here is what to expect so you can walk into a conversation prepared.

1. The DSCR itself

The debt service coverage ratio compares the property’s monthly rent to its monthly payment (principal, interest, taxes, insurance and any HOA dues). Many programs like to see 1.0 or higher, meaning the rent covers the payment. Some programs will consider a ratio below 1.0 with a larger down payment or different pricing. See our step-by-step guide to calculating DSCR.

2. Credit score

You are not asked for income, but your credit still matters. Higher scores generally earn better rates and higher leverage. Lenders commonly set a minimum score, and the best pricing tends to go to borrowers in the upper tiers. If your score is on the edge, it is worth asking what changes at each level.

3. Down payment and loan-to-value

DSCR loans are usually offered up to a maximum loan-to-value (LTV), which means you put some of your own money in. The maximum depends on the loan purpose (purchase, rate/term refinance or cash-out), the property, your credit and the DSCR. Cash-out refinances typically allow less leverage than purchases.

4. Cash reserves

Lenders often want to see that you have liquid funds set aside, commonly expressed as a number of months of payments. Reserves show that a vacancy or repair will not immediately put the loan at risk.

5. Property type and condition

Single-family homes, condos, 2–4 unit properties and some multifamily and mixed-use properties can all work, but each can price differently. The property generally needs to be in rentable condition. If it needs major work first, a fix and flip or bridge loan followed by a DSCR refinance is a common path.

6. Entity and documents

Most investors close in an LLC. Expect to provide entity documents, an ID, a lease or rent estimate (often supported by an appraisal), proof of insurance and proof of funds for the down payment and reserves. You generally will not need tax returns, W-2s or pay stubs.

7. Experience

Some programs ask about your investing track record, especially for larger loans or lower credit scores. First-time investors are often still eligible, sometimes with slightly different terms.
