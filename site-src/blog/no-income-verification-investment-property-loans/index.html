Investment Property Loans With No Income Verification

How investors buy and refinance rentals without handing over tax returns, and what lenders look at instead.

By the BridgePoint Lending team · October 14, 2026

If you are self-employed, write off a lot of expenses, or already own several properties, a traditional mortgage can be frustrating. Your tax returns may show far less income than you actually earn, and the paperwork can drag on for weeks. That is why many investors look for a loan with no income verification. For rental properties, the most common version is the DSCR loan.

What “no income verification” really means

It means the lender does not ask for your W-2s, pay stubs, tax returns or a debt-to-income calculation. It does not mean there are no requirements. The lender simply decides based on a different question: can this property pay for itself?

What lenders look at instead

  • The property’s rent. The loan is sized around the rent the property earns, or could earn, compared with its monthly payment. That comparison is the debt service coverage ratio.
  • Your credit. A credit report is still pulled, and your score affects your rate and how much you can borrow.
  • Your down payment. You will put money into the deal. The amount depends on the loan purpose, the property and your credit.
  • Cash reserves. Many lenders want to see that you have funds set aside to cover payments if the property sits vacant.
  • The property itself. Condition, type and location matter, and an appraisal is typically part of the process.

Who these loans tend to fit

  • Self-employed investors whose tax returns do not reflect their real cash flow.
  • Investors who already have several mortgages and are running into debt-to-income limits.
  • People who want to close in an LLC rather than in their personal name. See buying a rental in an LLC.
  • Buyers who want a simpler document list and a faster timeline than a bank usually offers.

The trade-offs to understand

Skipping income documentation is a convenience, and lenders price for it. Compared with a conventional loan, DSCR financing often comes with a somewhat higher rate, a larger down payment and a prepayment penalty on some programs. Whether that is worth it depends on how much the speed and flexibility are worth to you. We compare the two in DSCR vs conventional loans.

What if the rent does not cover the payment?

A ratio at or above 1.0 is the easiest path, but it is not always a hard stop. Some programs will look at a lower ratio with a larger down payment or different pricing. A higher interest rate or a longer amortization can also change the math. A loan officer can show you what moves the number for your specific property.

What you will typically still provide

  • A government ID and the entity documents if you are buying through an LLC.
  • The purchase contract, or your current mortgage statement if you are refinancing.
  • A lease, or a rent estimate supported by the appraisal.
  • Proof of insurance, and proof of funds for the down payment and reserves.
