DSCR vs Conventional Loans: Which Is Right for Your Rental?

Two very different ways to qualify for investment property financing.

By the BridgePoint Lending team · October 4, 2026

When you buy or refinance a rental, you will usually choose between a conventional loan and a DSCR loan. They solve the same problem differently. Here is how to decide.

The core difference: whose income counts

A conventional loan is underwritten on you: your income, employment history, tax returns and debt-to-income ratio. A DSCR loan is underwritten on the property: how its rent compares with its payment. See what a DSCR loan is for the basics.

When a conventional loan makes sense

  • You have strong, easily documented W-2 income and few properties.
  • You want the lowest possible rate and are fine providing full income documentation.
  • You plan to hold the property in your personal name.

When a DSCR loan makes sense

  • You are self-employed or your tax returns do not show your real earning power.
  • You already own several properties and are hitting conventional limits.
  • You want to buy in an LLC and keep the deal separate from your personal finances.
  • You need to close fast and keep documentation light.

The cost question

DSCR loans usually carry a higher rate than conventional financing. Many investors accept that tradeoff for speed, simplicity and scalability — and refinance later if their situation changes. What matters is the whole picture: rate, fees, down payment, prepayment terms and how the loan fits your plan.
